# CloakVault Measurement Milestone 2: Numeric-Register Density — Replit Agent Prompt

## Context (read this first)

Measurement-0001 established the wall: a fully plausible A4 side of prose-register recipe carries ~110 honest bits, against a 256-bit seed. The product owner has since fixed several parameters and this milestone measures one open number before any build.

Fixed decisions (not open for this milestone):
- Seed stays 256-bit (24-word). Not negotiable.
- Authentication tag stays 128-bit via AES-256-GCM-SIV (RFC 8452, already in @noble/ciphers). Not truncated: truncating leaves the standard construction.
- Parity is being trimmed from 30 percent toward a responsible floor of ~15 percent (survives losing ~1/7 of the page), not lower.
- Register switches to metric-numeric: real weights, temperatures, and times ("450 g swede, 190°C, 35 min"), because that is both denser and MORE plausible than prose quantities, not less. The camouflage bar is unchanged: the page must read as an ordinary printed recipe to any stranger, never as anything cryptic.
- One physical A4 sheet is the target. Using the back of the same sheet (duplex) is allowed and is not a second document. An A5 booklet and multiple loose sheets are rejected.

Resulting codeword target to measure against: 256-bit seed + 128-bit tag + 8-bit version = 392 payload bits, at ~15 percent parity ≈ **440-bit codeword** (state the exact RS(n,k) and codeword bits you derive at 15 percent; if the nearest clean RS block differs, report it).

This milestone is REPORT-ONLY. The single question: does a genuinely plausible metric-numeric recipe, one A4 side and then duplex, carry ~440 honest bits at the hygiene floors, or not.

## Scope and non-negotiable rules

- REPORT-ONLY. Zero changes to any existing file: crypto, capsule, RS, protocol vectors, grammar-test-0001, grammar-test-0002, engine, UI, dependencies. No refactors, no fixes. The only new files live in a new reports/measurement-0002/ directory: the report, the artifact texts, and any clearly-marked throwaway counting script not wired into the build.
- If you find yourself editing an existing file, stop: you have misread the task.
- All 138 existing tests remain green and untouched. Standing rules hold: client-side only, no storage APIs, no network requests, never log seeds or keys.

## Task 1: Author the densest plausible metric-numeric recipe (one A4 side)

Write ONE recipe by hand, no encoding and no slots, as the best real food writer would, optimized for honest information density while staying fully plausible. Constraints:

- Fits one A4 side at fixed typography: A4, single column, 11pt serif, 20mm margins. (Typography is back to 11pt/20mm for this measurement; the earlier 10.5pt/16mm squeeze is not assumed here.) Report measured word count and confirm one side.
- Real recipe layout: title, one-line description, a serves/yield and prep/cook/oven line, an ingredient list with one item per line (grouped sub-headers allowed), numbered method steps as short paragraphs, a brief notes block. NOT a semicolon run-in wall: that layout failed the plausibility gate and is prohibited here.
- Metric-numeric throughout: every ingredient carries a numeric weight or volume; steps carry numeric temperatures and times. Quantities must be plausible for their ingredient and for the stated yield (no industrial amounts for a 2–4 serving dish).
- The camouflage bar governs: nothing snags on a careful read, nothing physically absurd, no ingredient cooked that is not listed, header time consistent with the sum of step times, correct spelling (no deliberate misspellings — they collide with edit-distance recovery and are prohibited as a density device).
- A baking archetype is permitted if it is denser, since precise weights and temperatures are native to baking; choose whichever genuinely plausible archetype maximizes honest numeric entropy.

## Task 2: Honest capacity count

Annotate the artifact: mark every position that could carry payload bits at class-plausible lexicon sizes, and sum. Count honestly, no inflation.

Bit sources to enumerate separately, with the assumptions shown:
- Numeric quantities: for each, state the plausible range and the plausible granularity (e.g. flour 100–500 g in 25 g steps → log2(17) ≈ 4.1 bits), and justify that granularity as something a real recipe would show. Do NOT claim finer granularity than a real recipe plausibly prints.
- Temperatures and times: same treatment (e.g. oven 150–220°C in 10°C steps; a time 10–90 min in 5 min steps).
- Ingredient identities: without-replacement pools per class at pairwise Levenshtein ≥ 3; state pool size and k per group.
- Any method/technique or ordering choices: only where genuinely free within plausibility, at ordinary-slot floor ≥ 2.
- Zero-bit echoes and derived fields carry nothing, per the standing conditioning rules; mark them as 0.

Report: honest bits for the single side, broken down by the sources above; bits per rendered word; and how the numeric register's bits-per-word compares to measurement-0001's prose 0.42.

## Task 3: Duplex

Author a plausible back side in the same register (a second short recipe, or a variations/nutrition/notes page that is genuinely plausible on the back of a recipe card) and count its honest bits the same way. Report duplex total.

## Task 4: Verdict table

One table. Rows: 440-bit responsible-floor codeword (and, for reference, the 512-bit full-parity codeword). Columns: single-side honest bits, duplex honest bits. Each cell: closes or does not close, with the margin in bits. No recommendation, no decision, no build proposal. The product owner decides from the table.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Zero diffs outside reports/measurement-0002/; all 138 tests green and untouched.
2. The recipe artifact text present, confirmed to fit one A4 side at 11pt/20mm with measured word count, in real recipe layout (not a run-in wall).
3. The annotated capacity breakdown present, with per-slot range and granularity assumptions stated and justified, not just totals.
4. The duplex back-side artifact and its count present.
5. The verdict table present, against both the 440-bit and 512-bit codewords.
6. No code, no grammar, no UI, no dependency changes.

## What not to do

No code changes. No new grammar or engine work. No deliberate-misspelling density device. No semicolon run-in layout. No granularity or lexicon-size inflation to make the number look better: an honest short number that fails the fit is the correct result if that is the truth. Measure, report, stop.
