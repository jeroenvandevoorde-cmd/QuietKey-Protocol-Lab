# CloakVault Milestone 4 — OCR Fix (root-caused)

## What this is

Correcting the diagnosis of the garbled OCR. The physical print is clean: laser-printed, flat, sharp, evenly lit, on white paper, and the recipe text is fully human-legible end to end. The garbling is not a print, camera, or lighting problem. It is the app's tesseract.js misreading a clean scan. Evidence: the joined OCR output injects phantom characters exactly at line starts and boundaries (leading "E -", "F -", "; ~ -", "| -", stray digits, "6 parsn D8 gang"), while the actual words between them read correctly. Page confidences were 81 to 89 percent, which is mediocre for text this clean.

This is a tuning problem, not an OCR-capability wall, and not a concept risk. Do not change the crypto, the cloak engine, the RS layer, the grammar, or the vector package. All changes are in the printable-output renderer, the Tesseract configuration, and the tokenizer's robustness.

## Root causes (both must be addressed)

1. **Leading list markers.** Every ingredient and note line begins with "- " and every section with "## ". Tesseract turns a hyphen-space at the left margin into junk glyphs (E, F, |, ;, ~) and reads "##" unreliably. Every phantom in the output sits exactly where a line-leading marker is. This is the primary culprit.

2. **Untuned Tesseract.** tesseract.js is running with default page segmentation and no character restriction, on content drawn from a small fixed lexicon where both can be constrained hard.

## Required fixes

**R1. Marker-free printable output.** The printable document and the .txt export must contain no leading "- " on lines and no "## " on section headers. Emit clean lines: the section label as a plain word ("Ingredients", "Instructions", "Notes", "Variations") and each item as its bare text with no leading punctuation. The decoder anchors slot positions on the fixed connective prose and lexicon words, not on bullets or hashes, so removing the markers loses no payload and no alignment information. Confirm in code that the tokenizer does not depend on "- " or "##" as anchors; if it does, re-anchor on prose only.

**R2. Tesseract configuration.** Set page segmentation mode to PSM 6 (assume a single uniform block of text). Restrict recognition with a character allow-list covering only what the document can contain: lowercase a-z, the digits and characters that appear in numerals and connective prose, space, comma, period, and the pipe used by the "Serves ... | Prep ... | Cook ..." line. Everything outside that set is exactly the phantom glyph class to exclude. Keep the per-photo confidence display.

**R3. Tokenizer robustness to stray tokens.** The decoder must tolerate stray non-lexicon tokens appearing between slots without shifting subsequent slot positions. A phantom glyph or an unexpected token must become a skip, never a slot displacement, because a displaced slot corrupts alignment for everything after it and is worse than an erasure. Verify the connective-prose anchoring holds when junk is interleaved.

## Tests to add (M4 gate)

1. **Clean-render OCR test.** Render a known document marker-free, rasterize it to an image at print-like resolution, run it through the configured Tesseract path, decode, and assert erasures are under the 28-byte budget and the fingerprint matches. This closes the loop the automated suite currently skips.

2. **Junk-injection tokenizer test.** Take a known-good document, inject random non-lexicon characters and short tokens at line starts and between slots, confirm recovery still succeeds and slot positions do not shift.

3. **Marker-absence assertion.** Assert the printable/.txt output contains no line-leading "- " and no "## ".

## Interim path (do this in parallel; it yields the number now)

The decoder reads the editable textarea, not the image, so the physical recovery number can be obtained immediately by hand-cleaning the current OCR output:

1. On the existing garbled two-page OCR result, delete the phantom leading glyphs and repair the few mangled tokens so the textarea matches the printed document.
2. Run Recover with that run's Vault Key.
3. Report diagnostics: word erasures, byte erasures, bytes corrected, and fingerprint match.

This separates "are the words legible and recoverable" (expected yes) from "how accurate is raw OCR before correction" (the tuning number R1 and R2 improve). Report both: the hand-cleaned recovery result now, and the post-fix raw-OCR erasure counts once R1 to R3 land.

## Out of scope

No changes to crypto, KDF, AEAD, RS, the cloak grammar, grammar-test-0002, or the frozen vector package. This is OCR-pipeline and printable-output work only. Do not mark M4 complete until the clean-render OCR test passes and a real photographed two-page laser print recovers with erasures under budget.
